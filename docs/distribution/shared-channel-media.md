# Channel selection and shared media

Curators select destinations independently of speaker branding. The playlist setup shows its channel choices by default, and individual-master generation offers the permitted channels as checkboxes. Existing started playlists show their saved settings; changing a speaker no longer clears or forces channel selections. The Bhagavat Katha staging batch was started with YouTube/English and must not be silently restarted or broadened.

Before any files have been generated, the Curator can choose **Pause and change channels**. This atomically fences the playlist's old generation jobs and pauses its unstarted members. Saved masters and analysis checkpoints remain intact. **Save channels and resume** applies the confirmed permissions and destinations to the same unfinished packages and remaining recordings. Generated, reviewed, approved or reused packages are protected from this settings change. An active transcription must reach its saved checkpoint before pausing; ordinary retry actions cannot silently resume a paused plan.

## Reuse rules

The generator shares one render for matching source ranges, language/cues, media edits, screen/framing/art decisions and closing destination. A title distinguishes renders only when an opening card actually embeds it. Posting text and thumbnail titles remain independent. Every destination still receives its own output ID, source evidence, human workflow and publication record. Different excerpts, captions, audio/framing edits or embedded cards create different media.

Identical generated bytes are stored once within a single master version and package under a checksum-addressed `shared` key. Output descriptors retain their own validated logical `identityKey` and channel-specific download filename. Physical objects remain private and immutable. A retry verifies existing bytes instead of writing another copy; corrupt objects fail closed. Cross-package or cross-source sharing is forbidden. Cleanup counts shared bytes once and continues to protect referenced media. Old output-specific keys remain supported; this change neither migrates nor regenerates existing Shorts.

Facebook Reels via Buffer currently require 9:16 video, 3–90 seconds; YouTube Shorts support square/vertical video up to three minutes. The common Studio video is 1080×1920 H.264/AAC MP4. A compatible complete teaching can serve both. Duration checks include the closing card. The planner must choose a complete shorter teaching when Facebook cannot accept a longer YouTube excerpt, never truncate a thought just to reuse a file.

Regression coverage includes actual FFmpeg generation for all four portrait destinations with one render and one MP4, separate output names, alternate-speaker video/image/blog branding, local and S3 shared-object integrity, invalid aliases and cross-package bindings, source edits creating new content, repair/closing-refresh behavior, empty channel selections, preservation of selection across speaker changes, and independent per-file review/posting rules.
